# Public-sector variant

The same playbook at a government client (a city, an agency, a ministry) changes in five places.

| Topic | Enterprise (this case) | Public sector |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Margin, waste, cost | Public value: service time, equity of access, citizen trust, staff capacity; cost still matters |
| Differentiation | Against competitors | Against the status quo and other administrations; the question becomes "where can we serve citizens better, and where do we share solutions with other administrations?" |
| AI Act | Mostly minimal and transparency tiers | More use cases near Annex III (access to public services and benefits, point 5(a)); deployers that are bodies governed by public law must carry out a **fundamental rights impact assessment** (Art. 27) before using a high-risk system |
| Transparency | Customers informed | Often an algorithm register and explainability towards citizens; administrative law (motivation of decisions) |
| Buying | Procurement with a vendor shortlist | Public procurement rules: market consultation, tender timelines; framework agreements; plan the pilot within what can be procured |
| Governance | AI council under the CIO | Political accountability: the council reports to the management board and, for sensitive uses, to the elected body |
| Embedded work | Head office | Often inside the administration's own teams for months; working with their rhythms (budget cycle, council meetings) |

## What stays the same

Readiness before tools, triage of existing efforts, criteria before candidates, pre-registered pilots with a comparison group, production gates, and human-first principles. They matter more, not less, when the people affected are citizens.
