# Your AI orchestration layer is an operating model, not a product

*Draft article for the studio's blog. ~900 words.*

Every large organisation we meet has the same AI estate: thirty to forty initiatives, a handful in production, a few measured, and some tools paid for on a personal credit card. Leadership asks for "an AI platform" to bring order. Vendors are happy to sell one.

The platform is necessary. It is not sufficient, and buying it first is often the most expensive way to discover that.

## What scattered AI actually costs

At one retailer, the inventory found 37 initiatives costing about €45,000 a month. That sounds manageable until you look at what the money bought: two teams solving email triage with two vendors, two recipe recommenders, a CV-screening feature switched on by an HR vendor that nobody had assessed, and customer data pasted into free tools for campaign copy. The direct cost was small. The real cost was attention and risk: dozens of things to steer, and no way to stop any of them.

## Three things an orchestration layer has to do

**1. Make the right way the easy way.** A gateway in front of approved models, with single sign-on and a cost line per initiative, is the technical part. The organisational part is a fast lane: a low-risk request answered in five days. If the sanctioned route takes five weeks, the credit cards come back.

**2. Make every AI use visible.** A register with an owner, a risk tier and a KPI for every system, including the AI inside SaaS products that never touches your gateway. The CV-screening feature above would have been caught by procurement asking one question.

**3. Make stopping normal.** Triage rules agreed with the CIO and CFO *before* anyone sees their own result: scale, fix, merge, pause or stop, each with a one-sentence reason. Twelve initiatives stopped or merged was the most liberating outcome of that engagement.

## The order matters

We now start with the rules and the register, then the gateway, then the more advanced parts (tool registries, evaluation harnesses). Governance in month one, a gateway in month two, evaluation in month three. The technology arrives into an organisation that knows what it is for.

## How you know it works

Not by the number of pilots. By three numbers: the share of AI spend with an owner and a KPI, the time from a request to a decision, and the number of initiatives stopped. If the last one is zero after a year, nothing has changed.
