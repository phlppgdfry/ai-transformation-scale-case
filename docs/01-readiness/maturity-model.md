# AI maturity model

Seven dimensions, five levels. Each dimension has four statements that respondents score from 1 to 5; the statements are in [`data/assessment.json`](../../data/assessment.json).

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Individuals try tools. No policy, no inventory, no measured value. |
| 2 | Experimenting | Pilots in several units, some funded. Little reuse, value anecdotal, governance reactive. |
| 3 | Defined | Policy, intake and inventory exist. A sanctioned platform. Pilots have KPIs and a path to production. |
| 4 | Scaled | Several AI solutions in production with tracked benefits. Shared platform and components. Literacy across the workforce. |
| 5 | Embedded | AI is part of how processes are designed. Portfolio managed like any other investment. Continuous improvement on measured outcomes. |

| Dimension | What we look at |
|---|---|
| Strategy and leadership | Stated ambition in measurable terms, portfolio funding, executive owner with a mandate to stop |
| Governance and risk | Policy, inventory, intake, AI Act and GDPR assessment per system |
| Data foundations | Timely, trusted operational data at the right grain; ownership; access |
| Technology and platform | Sanctioned access to models, monitoring, reusable components, vendor security |
| Skills and talent | Literacy for users, building capability, product ownership, champions |
| Ways of working and adoption | Frontline involvement, production thinking from the start, change management, calibrated trust |
| Value management | Baselines and outcome KPIs, benefit tracking, stopping what does not deliver, cost visibility |

## How we score

- A respondent's score per dimension is the mean of the four statements.
- A business unit's score is the mean of its respondents.
- The group score gives **each business unit equal weight**, so 8,200 store employees do not drown out 450 people in e-commerce. (Weighting by headcount is a legitimate alternative; we show both views to the executive committee.)
- The spread within a business unit is reported: disagreement is itself a finding.
- Survey scores are checked against evidence from interviews and documents. A unit that scores itself 4 on "benefits are tracked" but cannot show a benefits report is scored on the evidence.

## Why seven dimensions and not one score

A single maturity number hides the decision. Ardena's group score is a 2.3, but the data foundations in supply chain are at a 3.0 while governance everywhere is below 2. That difference decides the order of the roadmap.
