## What the scores say

1. **Governance is the largest gap (<!--n:rd.group.governance-->1.8<!--/n-->, gap <!--n:rd.gap.governance-->1.7<!--/n-->).** No unit has an AI policy that staff use, no inventory, no intake. This is why people pay for tools themselves: there is no legitimate way to get one quickly.
2. **Value management is next (<!--n:rd.group.value-->2.1<!--/n-->).** Of <!--n:inv.total-->37<!--/n--> initiatives, only <!--n:inv.measured-->11<!--/n--> have a measured result. Nobody stops anything.
3. **Data is uneven.** Supply chain and e-commerce have usable data (around 3); stores and head office do not. The fresh markdown pilot depends on store-level waste data, which is why the retail data platform (F1) is on the critical path.
4. **E-commerce is a unit ahead** (<!--n:rd.bu.ecom-->2.8<!--/n-->): it has skills, a platform and a habit of measuring. It is the natural source of champions and of reusable components.
5. **Stores are furthest behind** (<!--n:rd.bu.stores-->1.8<!--/n-->), and they are where the largest value is (fresh, ordering, staff time). The roadmap has to bring capability to stores, not wait for stores to build it.

## What this means for the roadmap

- Governance and literacy start in month 1, because they unblock everything else and cost little.
- The orchestration layer (F2) replaces shadow tools with a sanctioned route within four months.
- The retail data platform (F1) is funded centrally: it serves the three highest-value use cases.
- Every use case enters the roadmap with an outcome KPI and a baseline (value management).
