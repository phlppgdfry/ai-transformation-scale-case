# Change and adoption

Most AI programmes that stall do so on adoption, not on technology. Stores are the hardest and most valuable place: 8,200 people, low readiness (<!--n:rd.bu.stores-->1.8<!--/n-->), little time.

## Approach (ADKAR per audience)

| Audience | Awareness | Desire | Knowledge | Ability | Reinforcement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Store fresh teams | Store visits by the head of fresh; why waste matters | Less waste, less guessing, no extra admin | 30-minute training on the handheld | Champion per store for the first month | Weekly waste per store visible; feedback used |
| Store managers | COO's message; pilot results from peer stores | Their store's numbers | Session at the regional meeting | Exception reports, not more reports | Waste in the store scorecard |
| Office staff | Policy launch, intranet | Approved tools that are better than personal accounts | Literacy module, tool training | Champions, office hours | Usage dashboard; tips from colleagues |
| Managers | ExCo message | Fewer, better initiatives | Product-owner training | Intake fast lane | Benefits reported at the council |

## AI literacy (Article 4 AI Act)

| Group | Content | Format | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyone using AI | What AI is and is not; Ardena's policy; data rules; checking output | 30-minute e-learning | ~4,000 |
| Champions | Prompting, use cases in their team, how to escalate | Two half-days | 60 |
| Product owners | Writing success criteria; evaluation; working with squads | One day | 25 |
| Executives | Portfolio thinking; risk; reading an eval report | Two hours | 12 |

## Works council

Informed in week 3 about the programme and the human-first principles, and consulted on any use case that touches scheduling, workload or monitoring. The pilot agreement: no individual performance metrics from the markdown handheld; acceptance rates are reported per store, not per person.
